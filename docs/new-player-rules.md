# Other Gods: Rules and Tutorial

This rules guide teaches the game in the order a new player should learn it: first how to read the screen, then how to build a legal deck, then how a turn works, then how combat, reactions, and advanced card text fit together. It follows the current in-game behavior and UI prompts; when a prompt gives a more specific instruction, follow the prompt.

## 1. Start With The Goal

Other Gods is won by reducing your opponent's Followers to 0.

Followers are your life total. Each player starts with 100 Followers. You lose Followers when your God is attacked directly, when combat goes badly for you, or when a card effect says so.

Your first job is simple:

- Protect your own Followers.
- Build a board that can attack safely.
- Use cards and Powers to create one strong turn where your opponent cannot stop the damage.

## 2. Learn The Main Menu

From the main menu, use these entries:

- Deck Builder: build and save legal decks.
- Tutorial: open interactive lessons that walk through the game step by step.
- Rules: open this full rules and tutorial reference.
- Practice vs Thor: play a local practice game when that mode is enabled.
- Multiplayer: choose a saved legal deck, create or join seeks, and watch live matches.

If you are new, open Deck Builder first, make or autofill a deck, save it, then play Practice vs Thor before going to Multiplayer.

## 3. Read A Card

Every card has a name, card type, culture, cost, and rules text.

Creature cards also have:

- STR: Strength. Used when attacking and when fighting aggressive Creatures.
- RES: Resilience. Used when defending in Defensive stance.
- SPD: Speed. Used for reaction timing, interception, and several card abilities.
- LVL: Level. Used by deckbuilding, summoning costs, and card effects.

Spells, Hexes, and Charms usually show Speed instead of creature stats. Their Speed matters when they respond to other actions.

Many cards also have subtype words such as Mage, Warrior, Dragon, Spirit, Aerial, Norse Creature, or Ancient Creature. These are not just flavor. Card effects often care about them.

When a card effect uses division or asks for half of a value, round down unless the card specifically says otherwise.

## 4. Know The Card Types

Gods start on the board in your God Slot. They are your identity for the match and often define your deck's culture and plan. Gods cannot be attacked as normal Creatures. Attacks aimed at a God damage that player's Followers.

Powers start face-down in your Power Slots. You pay mana to unlock them. Once unlocked, they provide passive effects or activated abilities.

Creatures are your main board presence. They attack, intercept, move, change stance, hold Equipment, and use abilities.

Spells resolve once, then go to the Graveyard unless an effect sends them somewhere else.

Hexes are prepared face-down on the board and usually trigger automatically when their condition is met.

Charms are reactive cards. Some can be prepared on the board, and some can be played from hand during priority windows.

Equipment attaches to Creatures, usually changing stats or granting abilities. Unequipped Equipment can sit on the board until picked up or destroyed.

Structures stay on the board and provide ongoing value. They can be attacked and usually use RES to survive.

## 5. Build Your First Legal Deck

A legal deck contains:

- Exactly 1 God.
- Up to 3 Powers.
- At least 40 regular cards.

Regular cards are Creatures, Spells, Hexes, Charms, Equipment, and Structures. Gods and Powers do not count toward the 40 regular-card minimum.

You may also register a side deck called **Reinforcements**:

- Its maximum size is one-third of your regular main-deck cards plus Powers, rounded down.
- Your God is not included in that calculation and cannot be placed in Reinforcements.
- For example, 40 regular cards plus 3 Powers allows up to 14 Reinforcements.
- Copy limits apply across the main deck and Reinforcements together.

Copy limits:

- Regular non-Legendary cards: up to 3 copies.
- A specific Legendary card: 1 copy.
- Total Legendary cards in the main deck: 1 for every 10 regular cards. A 40 regular-card deck can have up to 4 Legendaries.

Culture rules:

- Powers must match your God's culture or be Neutral.
- Some Gods have stricter culture-locked deckbuilding. If the deck builder marks a card unavailable, trust the warning.

First deck recipe:

1. Pick a God that looks fun.
2. Add 2 or 3 compatible Powers.
3. Add 16 to 20 Creatures so you can always contest the board.
4. Add a few Spells or Charms for interaction.
5. Add a few Hexes if you want defensive traps.
6. Add Equipment only if you have enough Creatures to carry it.
7. Press Save Deck when the deck builder says the deck is legal.

### Match Formats

Multiplayer seeks can be Bo1 rated, Bo1 unrated, Bo3 rated, or Bo3 unrated.

In Bo1, a single game decides the match. In Bo3, the first player to win two games wins the match.

After game 1 and, if needed, game 2, both players may move cards between their main deck and Reinforcements. The registered card pool stays the same, but the main deck does not need to stay the same size. The submitted main deck and remaining Reinforcements must still satisfy every normal deck construction rule before the next game can begin.

## 6. Understand The Battlefield

Each player has these zones:

- God Slot: your God.
- Power Slots: up to three face-down Powers.
- Frontline: five front lanes. Your Frontline is where attacks start.
- Reserve: five back lanes. Reserve Creatures are safer but usually cannot attack.
- Hand: cards you can play.
- Deck: your draw pile.
- Graveyard: destroyed and spent cards usually go here.
- Abyss: the exile zone. Cards in the Abyss are removed from normal play.

Think of each Frontline slot and the Reserve slot behind it as a lane. Lane position matters for movement, nearby Equipment actions, and many card effects.

## 7. Start Of Game

At the beginning of a normal match:

1. Each player places their God in the God Slot.
2. Each player places selected Powers face-down in Power Slots.
3. Each deck is shuffled.
4. Each player draws 5 cards.
5. Starting player is chosen.
6. The second player receives +2 mana as compensation.

You do not need to do this manually. The game sets up the match for you.

## 8. Your Turn Flow

Each turn starts by opening an Upkeep choice window before other normal actions. After that, the implemented turn flow moves through Main, Combat, and End.

Upkeep is the beginning-of-turn window. Your once-per-turn actions reset, upkeep effects happen, and you choose a resource option or a special upkeep option if one is available.

Main Phase is where you play cards, unlock Powers, activate abilities, summon Creatures, prepare Hexes or Charms, move Creatures, and set up attacks.

Combat Phase is where eligible Frontline Creatures attack.

End closes your turn and passes play to the opponent. If you have more than 7 cards in hand, the game asks you to discard down to the hand limit before the turn ends.

The most important beginner habit is to use Main Phase to build a board before making risky attacks.

## 9. Choose Upkeep Resources

During the Upkeep window, you usually choose one of these:

- Gain 5 mana.
- Gain 1 mana and draw 1 card.

On the very first turn of the game, the basic options are different:

- Gain 4 mana.
- Draw 1 card.

Choose mana when you have several cards you can play. Choose draw when your hand is weak, empty, or missing threats.

## 10. Spend Mana

Mana is used to:

- Play cards from hand.
- Summon Creatures.
- Unlock Powers.
- Activate abilities.
- Pay extra costs listed by cards.

Some cards also ask for additional costs such as discarding, sacrificing, banishing, or shelving cards. If you cannot pay every required cost, you cannot take that action.

## 11. Play Your First Creature

Creatures can be summoned to the Frontline or Reserve if the target space is legal and empty.

When you summon a Creature, choose a stance:

- Aggressive: face-up and ready to attack if eligible.
- Defensive: face-up and better at blocking.
- Stealth: face-down Defensive mode.

You may only perform one normal Creature summon per turn unless a card grants an extra normal summon. That normal summon spends one Minor Action from the summoned Creature. A Stealth summon also spends a Major Action.

Creatures do not have summoning sickness. If a Creature is otherwise eligible, it can act on the turn it enters the board. However, no Creature can attack on turn 1, and a Creature summoned after the first attack of the turn resolves cannot attack that turn.

## 12. Use Creature Actions

Each Creature normally gets:

- One Minor Action per turn.
- One Major Action per turn.

Minor Actions include:

- Move to an adjacent empty zone.
- Change stance.
- Pick up friendly unequipped Equipment in reach.

Major Actions include:

- Attack.
- Steal or destroy enemy unequipped Equipment when allowed.
- Use a major activated Creature ability.

Some cards grant extra actions or use special action rules. The card text wins when it gives a specific exception.

## 13. Learn The Three Creature Stances

Aggressive is the attacking stance. A Frontline Creature usually must be Aggressive to declare an attack. Aggressive Frontline Creatures can also intercept attacks aimed at Followers.

Defensive is the blocking stance. Defensive Creatures use RES when attacked and are better at intercepting.

Stealth is face-down Defensive mode. A Stealthed Creature is hidden from the opponent until revealed. A normal Stealth summon uses both the Creature's Minor Action and Major Action, so it is safer but more committal than a face-up summon.

Beginner rule of thumb:

- Put attackers in Aggressive.
- Put protectors in Defensive.
- Use Stealth when you want the opponent to guess what you prepared.

## 14. Prepare Hexes And Charms

Some non-Creature cards can be prepared face-down into an empty Frontline or Reserve slot.

Prepared Hexes are traps. They usually watch for a condition, then trigger automatically.

Prepared Charms give you a visible commitment to a future reaction. They can protect important lanes or threaten a response during combat.

Prepared cards occupy board space, so do not fill every lane with traps if you still need room for Creatures.

## 15. Unlock Powers

Your Powers begin face-down. To use one, pay its unlock cost.

Unlocked Powers can provide:

- Passive effects that stay active.
- Activated abilities you can use by paying their costs.
- Special timing options described on the card.

Power timing matters. If a Power helps your board every turn, unlock it early. If it is a surprise or finisher, wait until the turn you need it.

## 16. Use God Abilities

Your God often has a passive identity and sometimes an activated ability.

God abilities can shape the entire deck. A God that rewards Warriors wants many Warriors. A God that unlocks a special Active God wants a deck that can pay or enable that plan.

If your God has Champion's Call, it can summon that God's matching Active God. Shelving cards from your hand can help pay toward that summon cost when the ability says so.

## 17. Attack Basics

To attack:

1. Choose an eligible Aggressive Creature in your Frontline.
2. Choose a legal target.
3. The attack goes on the stack.
4. The opponent may respond or intercept if able.
5. The attack resolves if it is not stopped.

An attacker must be a Creature you control, in your Frontline, in Aggressive stance, able to spend a Major Action, not Sleeping, not blocked by a cannot-attack status, and not summoned after the first resolved attack of the turn. The game also blocks all attacks on turn 1.

Legal targets usually include:

- Enemy Creatures.
- Enemy Structures.
- The enemy God, which means damage to Followers.

Reserve Creatures are protected from many direct melee attacks. Use lane position, Reach, abilities, or effects to get around defensive setups.

## 18. United Front Attacks

If you have another eligible Creature, two Creatures can attack together as a United Front.

The attacking Creatures combine their Strength for that attack. This helps break through a large defender or deal a large burst of Follower damage.

Use United Front when:

- One attacker cannot destroy the target alone.
- You need one decisive attack more than several smaller attacks.
- You can afford to commit both attackers.

Be careful: committing multiple attackers can leave fewer Creatures ready to defend.

## 19. Interception

Before an attack resolves, the defending player may be able to intercept. An intercepting Creature steps in and becomes the defender.

Interception depends on protected target depth, interceptor row, stance, Speed, statuses, Reach, engagement restrictions, and card-specific special intercept hooks.

Core interception rules:

- Sleeping Creatures and Creatures with cannot-intercept statuses cannot intercept.
- The interceptor must be at least as fast as the declared attack, after intercept Speed bonuses.
- The interceptor must be able to engage the attacker. Incorporeal and similar restrictions still apply.
- Aggressive interceptors require a deeper protected target than Defensive interceptors.
- Frontline Aggressive Creatures can normally intercept attacks aimed at Followers or the God.
- Frontline Defensive Creatures can normally intercept attacks aimed at the Reserve, Followers, or the God.
- Reserve Defensive Creatures can normally intercept attacks aimed at Followers or the God.
- Reserve Aggressive Creatures normally need Reach or a special effect to intercept.
- Reach lowers the required protected-target depth.
- Special intercept abilities can override normal row, stance, and Speed limits.

Interception is how defensive boards keep Followers alive.

## 20. Resolve Creature Combat

Combat compares different stats depending on the defender.

Aggressive vs Aggressive:

- Attacker STR compares to defender STR.
- Higher STR destroys the lower STR Creature.
- The controller of the destroyed Creature loses Followers equal to the difference.
- If tied, both are destroyed.

Attacking into Defensive:

- Attacker STR compares to defender RES.
- If STR is greater, the defender is destroyed.
- If STR is lower, the attack fails. The attacker's controller loses Followers equal to the difference, and the defender's controller gains that many Followers.
- If tied, neither Creature is destroyed.

This is why Defensive stance is strong against reckless attacks.

## 21. Attack Followers Through The God

Gods are not normal combat bodies. When you attack the enemy God, you are really attacking their Followers.

The defender may still intercept if they have an eligible Creature. If they cannot intercept, the attack damages Followers.

A common win pattern is:

1. Force or remove interceptors.
2. Put a strong Creature or United Front in the Frontline.
3. Attack the enemy God for direct Follower damage.

## 22. The Stack

Many actions do not resolve immediately. They go onto the Action Stack first.

Examples:

- Playing a Spell.
- Activating an ability.
- Declaring an attack.
- Playing a reactive Charm.

When actions resolve from the stack, the most recent action resolves first. This is called Last In, First Out.

Simple example:

1. You play a Spell.
2. Opponent responds with a Charm.
3. Both players pass.
4. The Charm resolves first.
5. Your Spell resolves second if it is still legal.

## 23. Priority

Priority is the chance to respond before an action resolves.

After a player takes an action that uses the stack, the opponent gets Priority. Players can pass Priority back and forth. When both players pass consecutively, the top action on the stack resolves.

Responses usually must have Speed 2 or higher, and must be equal to or faster than the action they answer.

If the game prompts you for Priority, ask:

- Do I have a Charm, ability, or prepared card that helps right now?
- Is this action dangerous enough to spend a response?
- Can I wait and answer something more important later?

Passing is often correct. You do not need to respond to everything.

## 24. Speed

Speed appears on Creatures and on many reactive cards.

Speed affects:

- Whether a response is fast enough.
- Whether a Creature can intercept an attacker.
- Whether some attacks or abilities can target or engage a card.

Fast cards are good at forcing action. Slow cards are often stronger once they resolve but easier to answer.

## 25. Equipment

Equipment can be attached to Creatures to change stats or grant abilities.

Useful patterns:

- Put attack Equipment on a fast Aggressive Creature.
- Put resilience Equipment on a Defensive interceptor.
- Protect a key equipped Creature with Charms, Hexes, or lane position.

Unequipped Equipment on the board is vulnerable. Creatures may be able to pick it up with a Minor Action, and enemies may be able to destroy it with a Major Action.

## 26. Structures

Structures stay on the board and usually provide ongoing effects.

Structures have RES and can be attacked. If a Structure is central to your plan, defend its lane. If an enemy Structure is taking over the game, attack it before it generates too much value.

You may only summon one Structure per turn. Card effects that summon a Structure (rather than playing it normally from your hand) do not count against this limit.

## 27. Graveyard And Abyss

The Graveyard is where destroyed Creatures and spent cards usually go.

The Abyss is the removed-from-game zone. Cards with Void or cards hit by Void effects go there instead.

Some decks use the Graveyard as a resource with resurrect or return effects. Other decks use the Abyss to deny those plans.

## 28. Keywords And Status Effects

Cards explain their own rules text, and keyword hints appear in card detail views. Learn these common words first:

- Void: send a card to the Abyss.
- Immortal: cannot be destroyed.
- Sleep: the Creature cannot take actions or intercept.
- Mute: suppresses abilities; muted Powers cannot be unlocked or activated.
- Reach: improves interception range.
- Passive: always active while the card remains in play.
- Impact: triggers when the card enters the field from your hand.
- Universal Impact: also triggers when entering from other zones.
- Activate: use an activated ability.
- Unlock: pay to turn on a Power.
- Relock: turn an unlocked Power face-down again.
- Resurrect: return a destroyed card from Graveyard to field.
- Shelve: put a card on the bottom of its owner's deck.
- Mill: put cards from the top of a deck into the Graveyard.
- Search: look through your deck for a card, then shuffle.
- Dodge: cannot be attacked by lower-speed Creatures.
- Incorporeal: has special engagement restrictions.
- Slay and Fatality are combat-destruction triggers.
- Slain means a card was destroyed in combat.
- Destroyed triggers require destruction; sacrificing a card is not destruction.
- Perish triggers whenever a card is sent from the field to the Graveyard, including when it is sacrificed.
- Status: an ongoing condition on a card.

When a card and the general rules disagree, follow the specific card text.

## 29. A Safe First Turn

If you are going first:

1. Take mana unless your hand badly needs a card.
2. Summon one solid Creature.
3. Prefer Defensive or Reserve placement if the opponent can punish you.
4. Unlock a Power only if it helps immediately or defines your next turn.
5. End turn with at least one way to protect Followers if possible.

If you are going second:

1. Use the +2 mana advantage to catch up.
2. Answer the opponent's first threat or build a bigger one.
3. Keep enough defense to stop an early God attack.
4. Prepare a Hex or Charm if your hand supports it.

## 30. A Basic Midgame Plan

In the middle turns, your goal is to turn board presence into pressure.

Follow this loop:

1. Check your Followers and the opponent's Followers.
2. Check which enemy Creatures can intercept.
3. Decide whether you are attacking board, Structures, or Followers.
4. Spend mana before combat if it makes attacks safer.
5. Attack only when the result helps your plan.
6. After combat, leave defenders or reactions ready if the game allows it.

The best turn is not always the turn with the most attacks. It is the turn that leaves your opponent with the fewest good answers.

## 31. Multiplayer Flow

Before entering Multiplayer, save a legal deck in Deck Builder.

In Multiplayer:

1. Choose your saved deck.
2. Refresh seeks to view open rooms and live matches.
3. Create a seek if you want an opponent to join you.
4. Join an open seek if you want to play immediately.
5. Ready up when the room asks you to.
6. Use reconnect/resume controls if a match is interrupted.

Spectating live matches can be useful. Watch where experienced players spend mana, when they pass Priority, and how they protect Followers.

## 32. Practice Plan

For your first practice game, focus on one lesson at a time:

Game 1:

- Summon Creatures.
- Change stance.
- Attack the enemy God at least once.

Game 2:

- Try Defensive interceptors.
- Prepare a Hex or Charm.
- Practice passing Priority when you do not need to answer.

Game 3:

- Unlock all your Powers.
- Use at least one activated God or Power ability.
- Win by clearing interceptors before attacking Followers.

## 33. Common Beginner Mistakes

Do not attack a Defensive Creature with too little STR. If your STR is lower than its RES, you lose Followers and the defender gains Followers.

Do not leave Followers undefended when the opponent has Aggressive Frontline Creatures.

Do not fill every board slot with prepared cards. You still need Creatures to attack and intercept.

Do not unlock Powers just because you can. Unlock them when they improve this turn or the next turn.

Do not spend every response immediately. Save fast cards for actions that actually matter.

Do not forget board position. Row affects interception, while lane affects movement, nearby Equipment actions, and many card effects.

## 34. Full Match Checklist

At the start of your turn:

- What is my Follower total?
- Can the opponent kill me next turn?
- Which upkeep option do I need?

Before playing cards:

- What is my main threat this turn?
- Which enemy card stops that threat?
- Do I need mana for Priority responses later?

Before attacking:

- Which enemy Creatures can intercept?
- Are they faster than my attacker?
- Am I attacking STR or RES?
- What happens if the opponent has a Charm?

Before ending the turn:

- Can I protect my Followers?
- Are my key Creatures in the right stance?
- Did I leave a better action unused?

## 35. Quick Glossary

Aggressive: a face-up stance focused on attacking.

Abyss: removed-from-game zone.

Charm: reactive card, often playable during Priority.

Defensive: a face-up stance focused on blocking with RES.

Followers: your life total.

Frontline: the row where normal attacks are declared.

God: your identity card and the target for direct Follower damage.

Graveyard: normal discard and destruction zone.

Hex: prepared trap card.

Interception: a defender steps in to take an attack.

Mana: resource spent to play cards and use abilities.

Power: face-down deckbuilding card that can be unlocked.

Priority: a player's chance to respond before stack actions resolve.

Reserve: back row used for protection, setup, and some interception.

Stack: the ordered pile of pending actions.

Stealth: face-down Defensive stance.

United Front: two eligible Creatures attack together.

## 36. What To Learn Next

Once the basics feel natural, start studying individual cultures and Gods. Each God changes what a good deck looks like.

A strong player knows the universal rules. A dangerous player knows when their deck breaks those rules.
