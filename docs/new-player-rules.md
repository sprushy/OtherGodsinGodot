# Other Gods: Official Rulebook

Welcome to **Other Gods**, a strategic card game where players assume the roles of legendary Gods and command followers, creatures, powers, and spells to achieve victory. Your ultimate goal is to reduce your opponent's Followers to zero while protecting your own.

## 1. Objective and Resources

The game is won when you reduce your opponent's **Followers** to 0.

You start the game with **100 Followers**. They act as your life total. When your Followers reach 0, you are defeated. Followers can be lost when your God is attacked directly, or through the results of combat.

**Mana** is used to play cards, unlock Powers, and activate abilities. During your Upkeep phase, you choose between gaining 5 Mana or gaining 1 Mana and drawing 1 card.

## 2. Deck Construction Rules

A legal deck must contain exactly **1 God**, up to **3 Powers**, and at least **35 regular cards**: Creatures, Spells, Hexes, Charms, Equipment, and Structures.

You may include up to 3 copies of any regular card. You may only have 1 copy of a specific Legendary card. The total number of Legendary cards in your deck cannot exceed 1 for every 10 regular cards, so a 35-card deck can have up to 3 Legendaries.

Any Power cards in your deck must match the Culture of your chosen God, or be "Neutral".

## 3. The Battlefield

Each player uses the following zones:

- **God Slot**: Where your chosen God resides.
- **Power Slots**: Three slots where your chosen Powers are placed face-down at the start of the game.
- **Frontline**: Five lanes in the primary combat row. Only Creatures in the Frontline can declare attacks.
- **Reserve**: Five lanes in the back row. Creatures here are protected from direct melee attacks but can still intercept, use abilities, and move.
- **Hand**: The cards you can play. Maximum hand size is 7.
- **Deck**: Your draw pile.
- **Graveyard**: Where destroyed or discarded cards go.
- **Abyss**: The exile zone. Cards sent here are removed from the game.

## 4. Card Types

**Gods** start on the board. They have passive effects and/or activated abilities. Gods cannot be targeted in combat. Attacks against them deal damage directly to your Followers.

**Powers** are selected during deck building and placed face-down in your Power Slots. You must pay their Mana cost to unlock them. Once unlocked, they provide powerful passive or activated effects.

**Creatures** are your main forces. They have Strength (STR), Resilience (RES), Speed (SPD), and Level (LVL). Creatures can be summoned in Aggressive, face-up Defensive, or face-down Defensive mode. Face-down Defensive mode is called **Stealth**.

**Spells** resolve immediately and go to the Graveyard.

**Hexes** are traps that must be prepared face-down and activate automatically when specific combat conditions are met.

**Charms** are reactive spells that can often be played from your hand during your opponent's turn or prepared on the board.

**Equipment** attach to Creatures to boost stats or grant abilities.

**Structures** are permanents that provide ongoing benefits.

## 5. Turn Structure

### A. Upkeep Phase

At the start of your turn, once-per-turn actions are reset. You must then choose one of the following Upkeep options: gain **5 Mana**, gain **1 Mana and draw 1 card**, or use another available upkeep option.

### B. Main Phase

During your Main Phase, you may take actions in any order as long as you can pay the costs. You can play cards from your hand, unlock a face-down Power, or use activated abilities from your unlocked Powers, God, or Creatures.

You may only perform one normal Creature summon per turn. Hexes, Charms, and other eligible cards can also be played face-down into an empty Frontline or Reserve slot to prepare them.

### C. Combat Phase

You may declare attacks with your eligible Frontline Creatures.

### D. End Phase

Turn-ending effects resolve, and play passes to the opponent.

## 6. Creature Actions and Movement

Creatures **do not** have summoning sickness. They can attack, move, or change modes on the turn they are played if they are otherwise eligible to do so.

Each Creature can take a maximum of two Minor Actions per turn. Minor Actions include moving to an adjacent empty zone, changing mode, or picking up an unequipped Equipment card from an adjacent zone.

Each Creature can take a maximum of one Major Action per turn. Major Actions include declaring an attack, destroying an unequipped enemy Equipment card, or activating a major ability on the Creature.

Stealth is a face-down Defensive stance. Entering Stealth applies a **Speed penalty**, but it does **not** prompt Priority from your opponent.

## 7. Combat Mechanics

To declare an attack, choose an eligible **Frontline** Creature in Aggressive mode and choose a target: an enemy Creature, an enemy Structure, or the enemy God, which damages Followers directly.

If you have another eligible Creature, the two can attack together as a United Front and combine their Strength.

Before the attack resolves, the defending player has a chance to **Intercept**. An intercepting Creature steps in to take the attack instead of the original target.

Creatures in the **Frontline** can intercept based on stance. A Frontline Creature in **Aggressive** stance can intercept only attacks aimed at **Followers**. A Frontline Creature in **Defensive** stance can intercept attacks aimed at **Followers** or at the allied **Reserve** space behind or adjacent to it, according to normal lane adjacency. Creatures in the **Reserve** can intercept only while in **Defensive** stance, and only to protect **Followers**. Reserve Creatures in **Aggressive** stance cannot intercept at all unless they have **Reach** or another effect that specifically allows it. Some Creatures have effects that expand these rules. Speed matters: a slower Creature cannot intercept a faster attacker.

When two Creatures clash, their stats are compared based on the defender's stance:

- **Aggressive vs. Aggressive**: STR is compared to STR. The Creature with higher STR destroys the other. The controller of the destroyed Creature loses Followers equal to the difference. If tied, both are destroyed.
- **Attacking into Defensive**: The attacker's STR is compared to the defender's RES. If STR is greater, the defender is destroyed. If STR is lower, the attack fails, the attacker's controller loses Followers equal to the difference, and the defender's controller gains that many Followers. If tied, nothing happens.

## 8. The Stack and Priority

When a player takes an action, such as playing a Spell, activating an ability, or declaring an attack, it goes onto the **Action Stack**. Before the action resolves, the opposing player gets **Priority** to respond.

Responses must have a Speed of 2 or higher, and must be equal to or faster than the action they are responding to. Players pass Priority back and forth until both pass consecutively. The Stack then resolves in Last In, First Out order.

## 9. Keywords and Status Effects

Cards in **Other Gods** use many keywords and status effects. A few examples are:

- **Void**: The card is sent to the Abyss instead of the Graveyard.
- **Immortal**: The card cannot be destroyed.
- **Sleep**: The Creature cannot take actions or intercept.
- **Mute**: The card's abilities are suppressed and Powers cannot be unlocked.
- **Stealth**: The Creature is hidden face-down in Defensive mode until revealed.

There are more keywords in the game than the examples listed here. You can hover over keywords in a card's description to see what they do.

## 10. Game Setup

To begin the game, both players place their chosen God in the God Slot and their chosen Powers face-down in the Power Slots. Both players shuffle their decks and draw a starting hand of **5 cards**. A starting player is determined randomly.

The player going second receives a compensation bonus of **+2 Mana**. The first player then begins their turn.
