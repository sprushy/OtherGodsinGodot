# Bot Strategy Baseline And Card Comprehension

This document is the reference for improving the practice/ranked bots (`scripts/bots/ThorPracticeBot.gd`, `scripts/bots/BotCardStrategy.gd`, `scripts/bots/BotCardPromptPolicy.gd`). It has two halves:

1. A **strategy baseline**: the decision layers a strong Other Gods player runs every turn, mapped against what the bot does today, with the gaps called out.
2. A **card comprehension pass**: category-by-category and card-by-card notes on how each card should be used profitably, what the bot currently understands, and the concrete heuristic that would close the gap.

No code changes are included here; this is the planning baseline those changes should follow.

---

## 1. How The Bot Works Today

`ThorPracticeBot` is an event-driven state machine (signals → `_run_step`) with a single scalar economy. Everything is compared in points:

- `CARD_VALUE = 5000` — one card.
- `MANA_VALUE = 1000` per mana, with a saturation curve (`BotCardStrategy._mana_saturation_ceiling`) so mana past everything the deck can still pay for decays toward a floor; unbounded sinks (Ancient Pyre, Mana Guard) raise the floor.
  - Update (2026-11-17): the ceiling now also counts target-priced board abilities (`BotCardStrategy.board_ability_save_ceiling`). Nimue's Entomb charges the target's level, so a premium visible enemy body (e.g. a manifested Thor Active at Lvl 7) is spend potential even when today's mana is short — without this the bot spent down every turn and never banked enough to Entomb it, exactly the "worth saving for in a locked board state" failure.
- Followers are worth ~200/point above 50, ~350 between 25–50, ~600 below 25 (`_score_follower_loss`), lethal/lethal-prevention is `±CARD_VALUE * 20..30`.
- A card on board is worth `CARD_VALUE + projected_score/2`, discounted by disables (`_debilitation_discount`).

The turn flow:

1. **Upkeep** (`_choose_upkeep_option`): mana vs. draw, compared via best-affordable-creature lines, expected deck draw value (the bot knows its own deck composition), and god-specific overrides (Hermes lethal, Tonal Extraction, manifestation, Aphrodite).
2. **Main phase** (`_take_main_phase_action`): a cascade of plan generators — combat plan, Hermes/shift plans, removal setups, paired removals, power activations, summons (with attack-setup composition), manifestations, unlocks, defensive stance, equipment pickup, united front — sorted by score with lethal always winning.
3. **Combat model** (`_plan_combat`): BFS over attack sequences with dedup keys and `0.75^depth` discounting. Each attack candidate carries a base outcome plus every legal interceptor reply plus known prepared responses (`_get_known_attack_response_cards` models enemy The Inferno / Void Shield / Smite); the score takes the **minimum** over the defender's choices. Guard depletion, upkeep losses, and both players' follower totals are tracked along each line. The unmodeled fallback attack paths (`_try_attack`, supported attacks, move previews) must agree with this model: `_score_standard_attack_plan` always projects through the replies-aware candidates, and `_known_prepared_response_punishes_attack` blocks the followers/Askelladen/stealth/supported branches whenever a known prepared answer would fire, so execution can never submit an attack the planner refused on an unchanged board.
4. **Defense model** (`_forecast_incoming_follower_loss`): the opponent picks their best attack line against every legal target (followers, creatures, structures), the bot picks its best block/intercept, and one follow-up attack is re-checked with casualties and spent guard carried forward.
5. **Priority** (`_choose_priority_response`): responses are scored with the same `effect_score`/`target_score` tables used in the main phase, including buff sequences (`_score_combat_buff_sequence`).
6. **Prompts** (`BotCardPromptPolicy`): explicit per-trigger commands for ~30 selector prompts.

**What it already does well** (improvements should build on these, not replace them):

- Hidden-information discipline: `CardStrategy.visible()` gates nearly every read; the bot never peeks at stealth/face-down enemy cards, and wipe evaluation refuses to inspect hidden identities.
- Lethal detection across multi-attack sequences, removal-into-attack composition (`_plan_removal_setup`, `_plan_paired_removals`), and summon-into-attack composition.
- Opponent-reply modeling in combat: interceptors (with speed/row/stance legality via `match_manager._can_intercept`), three known traps, Jiang Ziya counterstroke, destruction guards, immunities, turn destruction wards.
- Mana economics: marginal rate, saturation ceiling from the bot's actual remaining deck, sinks that convert mana directly into follower swings.
- Locked-board detection (`_locked_board_state`) that turns Ancient Pyre's conversion into the win condition.
- Per-card rules for every non-creature card in the catalog (see §3).

## 2. Strategy Baseline: The Layer Model

A strong player runs five layers each turn. The bot executes layers 0 and 4 well, 3 partially, and barely runs 1 and 2.

### Layer 0 — Invariants (always true)

- Check lethal before anything else; check whether *you* die to the current stack first.
- Never attack into a Defensive body with insufficient STR (the rules' follower-loss-and-opponent-gain punishment is already priced by `_estimate_raw_attack_economy`, which now also tags the transfer so `_estimate_projected_attack_economy` credits the defender's controller with the attacker's final converted loss — halved, guard-spent, capped — and `_combat_outcome_with_remaining_guard` re-does the transfer with the recomputed loss for lines that spent guard).
- Spend mana only when the purchase beats the marginal mana rate.

### Layer 1 — Strategic posture (missing)

The bot has no concept of *what kind of game this is*. Add a posture evaluation once per turn (cheap, cached like `_locked_board_cache`):

- **Race clocks.** For each side estimate "turns to win at current rate": my best recurring unblocked follower damage vs. theirs (both already computable from `_get_combat_candidates` / `_forecast_incoming_follower_loss`), plus the **fatigue clock**: from turn 49 upkeep losses escalate every 7 turns (7, 14, 21…). `turns_to_fatigue_loss = followers / fatigue_loss(turn)` for each side. Three outcomes:
  - My clock < theirs and I can't lose to a burst → **RACE**: weight follower damage up (e.g., ×1.5 in `_score_follower_loss(opponent, …)` calls), accept worse card trades, attack followers over killing small bodies.
  - Their clock < mine → **DEFEND/DEVELOP**: weight danger forecast up, hold interceptors, prefer removal over tempo.
  - Locked board (already detected): if ahead on followers/fatigue, **do nothing risky** — passing wins; if behind, **force action** — the current Ancient Pyre exception should generalize to "any plan that breaks the lock," including bad trades that create an attack lane.
- **Win-condition inventory.** Once per turn, list "how do I actually win this game": follower damage / Ancient Pyre conversion / manifestation beats (Thor Active etc.) / token swarm (Hyena Pack, Harii Warband) / fatigue stall. Every scoring decision implicitly serves this list; today only Ancient Pyre gets this treatment.
- **Who's the beatdown.** Compare board strength + hand size + follower totals. The side with more tempo should attack followers and force answers; the side behind on board should trade and hold responses. Today stance and attack decisions are made purely from local economies, which produces the classic failure: the bot makes a "profitable" trade while it is the only deck capable of racing.

### Layer 2 — Resource ledger (partially there)

Mana is modeled deeply; the other resources are not:

- **Lanes** are a resource (10 per side). `CardStrategy.open_spaces()` exists but is only consulted by a handful of cards (Wild Magic, Drought, Palisade, Blot Sacrifice). Baseline rule: summon/prepare planning should reserve frontline slots for creatures when the board is contested, and prepared cards should claim reserve slots first (already the default via `_get_first_open_reserve_zone`) — but also: **don't prepare a second copy of a situational trap** (already implemented for Wheel of Fire; generalize), and **count enemy open lanes** when forecasting their next-turn summons (`_forecast_aggressive_exposure_risk` does this for stance; the danger forecast only sums existing attacker STR).
- **Actions** (minor/major per creature). The bot checks them per creature but never asks "how many of my majors are unspent at end of turn" as a global tempo metric. A cheap end-of-turn audit — any unspent major on an aggressive frontline creature with a legal attack means the attack planner said "not profitable" — is worth logging/tuning, because that is where race decks leak wins.
- **Cards.** `value()` treats all cards nearly equally. There is no "I'm card-poor vs. card-rich" term. Baseline: compute hand quality + deck velocity (draw options per turn); when card-poor, add a bonus to plays that draw/return cards and a penalty to plays that spend multiple cards for one effect; when card-rich (7+), convert cards to tempo freely.
  - Update (2026-11-16): body stats in `value()` are no longer flat `str * 80 + res * 25` (3.2x tilt). They price through meta-fitted reach/survival curves (`META_REACH_*` / `META_SURVIVAL_*` anchor tables in `BotCardStrategy.gd`; weight 23/point) fitted to the ranked self-play statline distribution: a str point buys the share of the field it can destroy, a res point the share of attackers that bounce off. An even 21/21 body went from ~2205 to ~2918 value points and Nimue (5/36) from ~1300 to ~2577; chip damage by small attackers (str <= 15) prices slightly lower, while the combat-relevant 21-27 band is unchanged. Poke income itself (`_score_followers_attack`, `_score_follower_loss`) is untouched - the fix raised what defense is worth on the other side of every comparison, it did not tax aggression. Re-fit the anchors if the ladder meta moves (dump/analyzer scripts in `scripts/tmp`).
- **Follower asymmetry.** `_score_follower_loss` handles pressure per point, but there's no *relative* term: losing 10 when the opponent is at 90 and I'm at 60 is much worse than the reverse. Add a race-differential weight (this is the Layer 1 hook in its cheapest form).

### Layer 3 — Threat assessment and opponent modeling (partial)

The one-and-a-half-ply danger forecast is good, but it only sees the current board plus a mana budget. Missing:

- **Opponent responses during my turn** are modeled for exactly three prepared cards (The Inferno, Void Shield, Smite) plus Jiang Ziya's counterstroke. A human holds charms/hexes in hand. Baseline: a "known responses" registry built from what the opponent has revealed (Mopsus reveals, Dellingr, sacrificed/destroyed cards seen this game) — the infrastructure (`CardStrategy.visible`, `_estimate_known_attack_response`) is already shaped for this; it just only ever consults the board.
  - Update (2026-11-16): protection this side *holds* now counts in its own risk math. `_get_own_protective_responses` mirrors the opponent-response scanner for Smite/Void Shield only (The Inferno hits the holder's board too, so it is not protective); sources are banked face-down zone copies plus affordable hand copies. Each ready reply halves `_forecast_aggressive_exposure_risk` (a clear attempt that eats the reply is a trade, not a loss), and a res-dominant body with a banked reply on an empty board now summons aggressive to poke instead of defaulting defensive. Already modeled elsewhere and left alone: glue/sleep ("cannot_attack"/"sleep" persisting to next turn) removes creatures from the danger-forecast attacker list, global attack restrictions (inactive Warding Stone) short-circuit both the danger forecast and exposure to 0. Still open: feeding own replies into `_forecast_incoming_follower_loss`/`_forecast_incoming_combat` so defend-posture math stops being pessimistic while a Smite sits banked.
  - Update (2026-11-23): the bot's own The Inferno no longer fires on a lone mediocre attacker while the follower pool is healthy. `_inferno_hold_charge` (applied alongside `sweeper_hold_value` in `_score_priority_card`) gives back the health-blind share of the avoided-follower-damage credit the generic response score pays — the `_current_turn_removal_safety` urgency clamp, so at 99/100 followers cancelling a 15-damage attack is priced at ~25% of its nominal value — then charges the prospect of a later, bigger trigger: visible enemy reserve bodies at half value (advancing costs a turn), hidden reserve bodies at the generic card price, plus one expected future deployment while the opponent can still field creatures, all taken at half because the opponent chooses whether to load the frontline. Both terms scale to zero as the pool empties, so a threatened board still fires the wipe to survive; a loaded frontline fires anyway because the fire-now gain is the whole frontline at once. This is the cross-turn sibling of `_attack_punish_hold_charge` (same-turn hold for Smite/Void Shield/Divine Lightning); The Inferno needs its own term because it re-fires on every future attack.
- **Enemy god abilities are invisible to planning.** Aphrodite's Violent Delights is modeled only for the bot's own Aphrodite. The unlock path is her OWN turn: her attack kills one of the bot's creatures, then she Enslaves the best remaining creature (front or reserve) for 5 mana. Modeling it in `_add_combat_casualty` fires on her attack (the engine's `combat_destroy_events_this_turn` clear at every turn start makes her activating after the BOT's attack impossible), so the bot's own combat kills during the opponent's turn do not charge it, and `_forecast_incoming_follower_loss` returns the risk so defensive decisions see it. Additionally, her hand is invisible, so when no visible stack can kill any of my bodies the exposure estimate still charges the freed value through the budget-bounded worst case (`_opponent_aphrodite_enslave_exposure`, ~half-weight pulse in `_aphrodite_exposure_pulse`). Same class of problem: Guan Yu tactical break, Freyja resurrect, Odin draw, Mummu shelve. Baseline: a per-enemy-god `opponent_threat_profile()` that annotates the danger forecast and attack replies ("this clean kill costs me X if they have Violent Delights").
- **Free/cheap summon lines** (Skoll upkeep summon, Hati moon hunt, Raven Storm sighting, Harii Jarl warband, Hyena Pack scavenge) are modeled only when the *bot* runs them. Facing them, the attacker forecast should add "opponent can add up to N extra bodies next turn at these costs."
- **Multi-turn accumulation.** Pazuzu drains 2 mana per turn, Baldr gains 5 followers per upkeep, Caleuche grows with each Spirit. None of these make the next-turn danger/mana forecasts. `get_projected_upkeep_follower_loss` is exactly the right hook — it exists, and only some cards implement it.

### Layer 4 — Tactical execution (strong, some gaps)

- **Sequencing**: summon-then-attack is discounted 25% for the response window (good); removal-then-attack, buff sequences, and paired removals are composed. Gap: the bot doesn't consider *attacking first, then summoning* to bait responses — acceptable to skip, but noted.
- **Positioning**: movement scoring (`_score_creature_move`) covers attack enablement, shelter, equipment reach, freed lanes, and interception coverage. Gap: **summon lane choice** is "first open zone" (front-left bias, `_get_first_open_summon_zone`); there is no "place my RES body where their biggest attacker can reach it / place attackers where their interceptors can't." A lane-assignment pass at end of main phase (score each friendly creature × each open/adjacent lane for interception coverage of the *actual* forecast threats) would be the single best positioning improvement.
- **Stance**: `_choose_creature_summon_mode` and the defensive/aggressive switch planners are reasonable; `_forecast_aggressive_exposure_risk` is a genuinely good heuristic. Gap: end-of-turn defensive setup only happens under danger or follower scarcity; against race decks the bot routinely ends the turn all-aggressive.
  - Update (2026-11-16, second pass): attack-safety now models interceptor absorption (`_is_attack_safe_from_interceptors`): a visible interceptor the attacker cannot destroy makes the attack pointless - the defender always chooses that trade, the declared target is never fought. This fixes attacks repeatedly thrown into an unkillable wall that intercepts. Stealth attacks are now first-class: filtered to clean kills (destroy + survive), cheapest sufficient attacker, most valuable stealth target, and attempted before standard attacks (a stealth body pokes un-interceptable every turn it lives). Poke-through stance: a body that can attack followers un-intercepted flips aggressive for it and is no longer turtle-flipped defensive after attacking. God manifestation (`_score_god_manifestation`) now charges the god-death follower loss plus the god's value at half weight when an existing attacker or affordable enemy summon out-stats the manifested body's defense in the planned stance - no more manifesting into a board that kills the god for nothing.
  - Update (2026-11-16): the old "empty opposing board -> always aggressive" summon rule is gone. Aggressive stance defends STR vs STR while defensive stance defends STR vs RES (`game_manager.gd` combat resolution), so a res-dominant body summoned aggressive throws away its better defense stat to poke with its worse one. Bodies with projected res > str summon defensive *only when they have no profitable work*: no clean kill, not the strongest projected body on board, and no enemy their combat math destroys (first pass charged only clean kills, which over-corrected strong res-tilted attackers like Kur-Jara 21/26 into passivity). With a banked Smite/Void Shield reply and an empty board the poke is exempted too. Hand-discard priority weights creature res at half of str (was 1/3) so walls are not the default discard.
  - Update (2026-11-18): the poke-through guard in `_get_defensive_mode_switch_score` is gone. It vetoed the defensive flip for any attack-spent body in an intercept-free lane while followers were plentiful, but it could only change an outcome when the casualty forecast showed that body destroyed in aggressive stance before its next attack - the one situation where the "recurring poke income" it was protecting cannot exist, since a dead poker never pokes again. An attack-spent body facing a concrete next-turn attacker (an advancing reserve creature) flips back to Defensive to survive; PreflightProbe's Nagual check pins that line.
- **Stealth**: `_should_summon_creature_stealthily` scans `ability_text` for the words "passive/impact/on summon" — a text-parsing holdover. It works, but it should be replaced by the same per-card reveal-value table `_estimate_reveal_trigger_value` already implements, extended with a "this creature's face-up passive matters" flag.

## 3. Card Comprehension Pass

### The comprehension ladder

A card is *understood* when the bot can answer five questions about it:

1. **Legal** — when can it be played/activated, and on what targets? (Always delegated to `MatchManager`/card authority — this is done well everywhere.)
2. **Valued** — what is the effect worth, in the shared economy?
3. **Timed** — is *now* the right moment, or should it be held (setup card, response card, late-game card)?
4. **Composed** — what other cards does it combine with (payment, sacrifice, buff, search targets)?
5. **Modeled** — when the *opponent* has it, what does it threaten, and how does that change my lines?

Most non-creature cards reach rungs 2–3; creatures mostly sit at rung 1 (bodies) with scattered rung-2/3 for ~35 of them; rung 5 is nearly empty except engine-level combat rules.

The gaps below are ordered by expected win-rate impact.

### 3.1 Creature activated abilities that are never activated (8 cards)

These are dead text when the bot plays them — the card is deployed as a vanilla body and its ability is never once used:

- **Berserker** — *Rage: once/turn, N mana: +STR and Immortal this turn; after any attack, Sleep until your next turn.* Profitable use: push a lethal or wall-breaking attack through, or make a trade where dying was the alternative (Immortal). Baseline heuristic: treat as a combat buff available to the attack planner (`combat_gain` with a sleep cost priced like Berserker Mead's −1500), usable on defense forecasts too (Immortal = no casualty).
- **En-hedu-anna** — *Exaltation (Major): if your God's power activated this turn while she was face-up, gain +7 RES/+7 STR/+1 SPD and cannot be attacked/destroyed/targeted until end of next turn.* She is a **combo piece**: play her, activate a power the same turn, then Exalt. Baseline: summon-score bonus when a cheap power activation is available this turn; value Exalt as a defensive stance-swap-plus (the "cannot be attacked" half is a superior version of shelter).
- **Gudud Priest** — *Creature Ward (Activate, Spd3): remove creature-applied effects from a creature; rest of turn unaffected by creature abilities.* A cleanse + combat-trick-proofing. Baseline: `cleanse_score` already exists (Gawain/Anointing Statue) — reuse it, plus a combat-reply value: casting it during an enemy buff-response makes the bot's attack immune to Charm/Hex stat tricks for that fight.
- **Harii Shaman** — *Transform: swap friendly Norse Warrior ↔ Animal subtypes.* Pure combo glue for Hunting Tactics/Norse Bloodlust decks (a Warrior can become a valid supporter/target and back). Baseline: value = "does this flip enable a Hunting Tactics pair or a Bloodlust buff that isn't currently legal?" — binary check against the bot's own power list.
- **Isimud** — *Forerunner: manifestations cost 6 less. Revelation (Major): summon a Divine Manifestation from hand for +2.* Baseline: summon-score bonus = 6 × marginal mana whenever a manifestation line exists in hand/deck, and a plan that spends his major action on Revelation when a manifestation is in hand and the god's Champion's Call is otherwise unaffordable this turn.
- **Mopsus** — *Seer (Minor): reveal an opponent hand card, +1 per friendly Avian.* Information is the weakest effect for a bot (it can't exploit reads), but the *registry* it feeds (Layer 3 known-responses) makes it real: value = number of unknown enemy responses currently unmodeled. If Layer 3 never lands, Mopsus stays near-zero and the ranked deck builder should treat it as a body.
- **Seventh Sage Utuabzu** — *Channel Ally (Impact)/Imbue Ally (Activate): re-trigger another Ancient Sage's impact.* Baseline: value = best other Sage impact on field/in Void (the sage impacts are already understood — Silence, Good Fortune, Search, Conjure Home), priced at the activation tax.
- **Winged Lion** — *Flank (Activate): move itself and another friendly creature to any friendly slots.* Free repositioning — the movement planner's dream. Baseline: value = best two `_score_creature_move` deltas it can create, evaluated in the same pass as `_try_improve_creature_position`.

### 3.2 Creature triggers with no summon/attack-time valuation (~12 cards)

The engine resolves these correctly once played; the bot just doesn't *know* it's getting them, so it mis-times or under-deploys:

- **Pazuzu** (Impact: −2 enemy mana; Passive: −2 enemy mana each upkeep) — should be summoned *before* the opponent's big turn, and when facing it, the bot's own mana forecasts must subtract 2/turn. Both directions missing.
- **Asakku** (on combat kill: +2 mana) — attack-economy bonus on kill lines, like the existing Masmassu mana valuation.
- **Aurboda** (Slay: Convert 7 followers) — attack-economy bonus on kill lines; also raises the creature's defensive value (trading up is a double reward).
- **Ankou, Servant to the Reaper** (Slay: draw a card) — kill lines should credit `CARD_VALUE ×` draw value; makes it a premium chump-blocker.
- **Rabisu** (Impact: Leech 3 STR/RES from each sleeping creature) — summon timing: play it when enemies are asleep (the bot already tracks sleep durations well for its own disables).
- **Gidim-Ensi** (Guardian: attackers of your followers Sleep; cannot attack) — a defensive wall whose *real* value is its Guardian trigger. `_estimate_defensive_summon_value` should add "attacks on followers now bounce into a sleep lock" when it's fielded.
- **Caleuche** (+10 STR/+1 SPD per other friendly Spirit) — stat passive flows through effective stats once on board, but summon-time comparison doesn't credit the delta, so the bot undervalues it in Spirit decks.
- **Raven Storm** (Spd 3: when a friendly Norse Human Warrior or Lupine attacks, summon it from hand for free) — the bot never does this; it's a free blocker/attacker every combat. Needs a hook in the attack planner: "declare attack X, then free-summon Raven Storm."
- **Pegasus** (Steed: a Human/Active God can wield it as equipment granting Spd 5 + Aerial + Evasive) — the equipment planner only handles `EquipmentCard`; Pegasus-as-gear is invisible. Add a steed branch to `equipment_score`.
- **Capricorn** (if sacrificed for a summon, +2 mana and return to hand) — the summon payment planner (`_build_creature_summon_payment_plan`) should treat Capricorn as a *discount* sacrifice (refund), making it the preferred payment card.
- **Wolf Cub / Wolf Adolescent** (Maturation: upkeep upgrade into a deck Lupine) — prompts are answered, but the summon score doesn't credit the upgrade chain (play a Cub early → it becomes a deck Lupine later), and the upkeep prompt decision isn't planned against danger.
- **Habrok** (Breakout: if it's your only creature at end of opponent's turn, return to hand + destroy their weakest creature) — the prompt always says yes, but the *strategy* (deliberately keeping it alone, attacking with it, replaying every turn) isn't modeled; worth a summon/attack-line bonus when the board is otherwise empty.

### 3.3 Creatures whose passive is engine-handled but under-*chosen* (selection/timing gaps)

- **Gullinbursti** (Dodge: slower creatures can't attack it) — engagement rules flow through, so the bot never makes an illegal attack; but it also doesn't *prefer* Gullinbursti as the attacker into slow blockers, or as the body that holds a lane against a slow board.
- **Edimmu / Gidim-Ensi** (Incorporeal: only Spirits/Mages engage them) — same: the matchup-aware summon bonus ("their board has no Mage/Spirit → this body is unkillable this turn") is missing.
- **Gilgamesh** (+STR per level deficit vs. higher-level creatures) — flows through combat math; missing as a *targeting preference*: he wants to attack up.
- **Tianlong, Holy Dragon / Urisk Grovekeepers** — The summon and follower-loss forecasts use the projected card's row and face-up state. Both price Reach and the separate speed exception when the target qualifies.
- **Minotaur Footsoldier / Telchine Apprentice / Low Light Troll / Seven-Headed Serpent / Asaruludu / Gala-Tura / Garm / Humbaba / Rabid Wolf / White Stag / Stone Monkey** — all resolve correctly; the gaps are valuation only (e.g., Garm should suppress the bot's own graveyard-line plans and boost its expectation against Freyja/Again Walker opponents; Rabid Wolf's disease should add a "survivor is crippled" term to fight scores; White Stag's Fatality giving the *opponent* 2 mana should slightly discount killing it via combat when the race is tight).

### 3.4 Non-creature cards: shallow-but-present understanding (timing/depth fixes)

All are "covered," but these have known shallow spots:

- **Dromi / Gleipnir** (bind hexes): scored as attack-lock/intercept-lock value; gap: they are best *pre-attached to the specific threat before combat*, and Dromi's drain-2-per-turn ticking end-game isn't priced (only an immediate 2×FOLLOWER_LOSS is).
- **Absence** (mute a power/God 4 turns): scored generically by target cost; gap: should prioritize powers that are about to pay off (counters, stored cards) over face-down randoms, and should be timed to the opponent's lethal/combo turn.
- **Circle of Rebirth**: evaluates `died_this_turn` only — correct, but the bot doesn't *create* lines where its own deaths feed it (acceptable; keep).
- **Watchtower** (reveal + counter enemies): flat 6000/3000 by enemy count; gap: doesn't distinguish which enemies (a counter on Fenrir/Jiang Ziya is worth far more than on a Bear).
- **Doorway to the Void**: values enemy graveyard recursion correctly (Again Walker/Huginn/Freyja bonuses exist) — this is one of the best-modeled structures; keep as the template.
- **Warding Stone**: unlock/play scored with attack-restriction awareness; gap: when the bot is *ahead* and racing, blocking its own attacks is a real cost the current score already handles via `attack_restrictions` — verify the reverse case (bot facing Warding Stone doesn't re-plan its attack lane after the restriction lands; the restriction map is only consulted in forecasts).
- **Ragnarok**: unlocked mainly as a panic reset; gap: the "I'm behind on board but ahead on hand" profile that makes it a fair card isn't in the posture layer yet — with Layer 1 in place its score can be posture-weighted.
- **Powers with activation depth issues**: `ManaGuard` guard sizing is excellent; `Palisade` pressure model is good; `AcceleratedFate`/`OraclesSight` are fine. **AnankesBinding/Breidablik/WalkOfTheSage/FireAndGold/DivineCaprice** (PLANNED_POWERS) each have real planners — the gap is *unlock timing* vs. the posture layer (e.g., Fire And Gold should wait for a ≥1-card-value enemy target, which it does, but only considers the current board).

**Item 11 — Mummu's Entropic Force economy (done, 2026-11-18).** The bot-as-Mummu never shelved for mana: `_plan_removal_god_ability` only fired Entropic Force as a follower-survival removal (`own and gain <= 0 → continue` skipped every own target that did not reduce the danger forecast), and the score charged the target's full economic value even though shelving returns the card to the deck. Observed symptom: three expired Warding Stones sat unshelved turn after turn. The Mummu branch now routes through `_plan_mummu_entropic_force`:

- **Cost is residual board value, not card value.** The shelved card cycles back through the draw, so the charge is half its *current* economic value — the same debilitation/doom/inactive-stone discounts that `_get_card_economic_value` already applies — while the mana pays at the marginal rate, so a saturated stock still refuses and a starved one pays full rate.
- **Devalued fuel reads as the ramp play it is.** Expired Warding Stones (restriction over, level 2 → +1 mana, cost ≈ the inactive-stone residual) shelve for near-pure profit; doomed bodies (`destroy_at_turn_end`, Tablet of Life's level-3+ resurrection) gain a rescue credit — the card banks into the deck instead of the turn-end graveyard; heavily debuffed bodies cost only their discounted value.
- **Temporary control ends on the bot's terms.** A borrowed body scheduled to return (`GameManager.is_temporarily_controlled`, new public helper over `_temporary_control_effects`) gains a denial credit (¾ of its value) plus the mana, and `SirensSong`'s `target_score` now adds `_mummu_temporary_control_bonus` while the god can still pay — a steal stops being a one-turn borrow when Entropic Force buries the borrowed card in its owner's deck bottom.
- **Funding plays and sequencing.** `_mummu_shelve_funds_summon` adds `ECON_CARD_VALUE/2` when the level mana converts an unaffordable hand creature into this turn's summon, and a target that still holds a safe, affordable direct attack is not shelved that pass (the plan returns empty), so the free attack submits first and the bank follows on the next planning pass. Doomed and borrowed targets are follower-safety neutral — they cannot cover the opponent's next attack anyway, since they leave at this turn's end.
- Verified by the new `_check_bot_mummu_entropic_force_economy` preflight fixture: the stone ramp resolves through the match command path (+1 mana, stone in the deck), a doomed Tablet summon is preferred over a healthy body, a borrowed body is preferred over stones, the summon-funding premium registers in the plan score, and a Siren's Song steal scores higher with a live Mummu than with him muted.

**Item 12 — E2-abzu's Void retrieval economy (done, 2026-11-18).** The bot voided Mer Mages through Walk of the Sage and then left them stranded: `_plan_e2_abzu_return` priced a retrieval on body stats alone (`_get_hand_value × 100`, so the impact Sages read as vanilla bodies and the +2500 summon kicker required a live same-turn summon), and the mana econ never banked toward the line because `_mana_saturation_ceiling` scanned only hand/deck/powers/field — a Mer Mage in the Void was invisible, so above the low ceiling every held mana point decayed as surplus the bot happily spent down each turn. **Item 10 — the held Altar of Dreams line: sleep source + coster = on-demand Void removal (done, 2026-11-18).** The Duat chain was the only sleep engine the Altar's valuation knew; a standing Alu (Stupefy), a Gambanteinn on a Mage bearer, or enemy bodies already asleep from any source make the same removal available every turn, and the bot neither banked mana for that window nor hesitated to burn its own bodies into the same summon.

- **`BotCardStrategy.controllable_sleep_source`** scans the bot's own ways to turn an enemy creature into Altar Void fuel, best first: a creature asleep right now (any source, no activation), a ready Alu or Gambanteinn-Mage (same turn, priced with its activation tax), the live Sleep of the Duat (fuel from the end of this turn), and the still-walking Duat chain (two turns out). `enemy_sleep_reachable` generalizes what `duat_reachable` used to gate, so `altar_unlock_line_gain`, `brood_take_line_gain`, and the Demon/Spirit deck-pick line all count any controllable source: an Altar unlock with a standing Alu and no sleeper on the field is priced as on-demand removal instead of zero.
- **The held line holds mana.** `CardStrategy.held_altar_line` (cached per decision step like the mana econ cache) packages `gross` (remaining Altar unlock + best executable coster's summon price + sleep activation tax), `need` (gross net of the upkeep income over the source's window — a live-now opportunity wants the stock on hand, a source a turn or two out can wait for its upkeeps), and `gain` (the best future Void target's value + the Abyss removal premium). `mana_penalty` charges competing plays that dip the stock below `need` a 30% share of `gain` — `ignore_altar_hold` exempts the line's own steps (the Altar unlock in `unlock_score`, the Gambanteinn sleep activation, and the coster summon, whose direct payment carries the burn charge instead) — `_score_creature_play_in_zone` applies the same charge to creature summons the light 12%-of-rate mana pricing would otherwise let strand the line, and `_upkeep_mana_enables_altar_line` takes the upkeep **mana** option when its income is what still closes the funding gap.
- **Direct sacrifice is weighed against the Void window.** Paying a Demon or Spirit coster with the bot's own bodies while no sleeper exists burns the window that summon could have used, so `_build_creature_summon_payment_plan` adds `held_altar_void_burn_charge` to the direct plan's penalty: the line's `gain`, halved while another executable coster keeps the next window open, discounted `0.8^turns` by the source's distance. The bot now waits for sleep-source removal instead of trading its board into the same summon — unless the immediate body is worth more than the removal it forecloses.
- `projected_upkeep_mana` was split into `projected_upkeep_income` + current stock so the need-netting reuses the same income model; the arithmetic is unchanged and PreflightProbe's Pazuzu assertion pins it.

**Item 13 — Siren's Song follow-through (done, 2026-11-23).** Observed symptom: the bot cast Siren's Song, then either did nothing with the borrowed body or switched it to defensive stance and passed. Three gaps compounded:

- **Pricing assumed attacks the bot cannot make.** `CardStrategy.combat_gain_for_stolen_creature` priced the steal by attack lines with no stance or row check, but the borrow preserves both: a defensive body arrives defensive (attacking needs a stance flip first — minor action plus stance mana), and a reserve-row body arrives in the back row where nothing attacks at all. The bot paid 3 mana for value it never collected. The scorer now gates every attack line (and the empty-board followers poke) on the body arriving able to spend its major action this turn — aggressive up front, flippable there with its minor action, or an aggressive reserve body whose advance can precede the attack — and charges the setup mana at the marginal rate. A defensive reserve wall now prices as pure denial (0), so `best_target` stops selecting it for attack value it will never submit.
- **The borrow was modeled as bot-side board presence.** `_forecast_incoming_follower_loss` counted the borrowed body as a bot defender and interceptor for the opponent's next attack window, even though Siren's Song returns it at this turn's end. That phantom defense is exactly what made `_plan_defensive_stance` flip a stolen body defensive and pass — handing the opponent their card back pre-defended. The forecast now drops temporarily controlled bodies from the defender pool and models an aggressive frontline loan as one of the opponent's returning attackers (the stance projection of a `{"stance": ...}` change follows it), so the danger read is the same before and after the steal. `_get_creature_attackers_next_turn` (stance savings, shelter pricing) makes the same correction, and forecast attack actions now carry `source_player`, with `_estimate_raw_attack_economy` attributing recoil and on-kill triggers to the declaring player instead of the card's current controller — a next-turn attack by a returning borrow no longer bills the bot for its own recoil.
- **No flip policy for a borrow.** `_get_mode_switch_score` charged aggressive-exposure risk for a body the bot does not own and cannot keep (its combat death is the owner's loss — see `_get_card_loss_owner`), and returned -1 for res-tilted bodies with no profitable line, so defensive steals just sat there. Borrowed bodies are now exempt from the exposure/casualty charges and always qualify for the "flip anyway" fallback: returning the body aggressive makes the owner spend their own stance change or defend at STR. `_get_defensive_mode_switch_score` and `_plan_defensive_stance` refuse borrowed bodies outright, so the two scorers cannot oscillate and the "switch to def and pass" line is gone. Mummu's Entropic Force shelve remains the one deliberate "hold the borrow" play (it ends the control and buries the card — see Item 11).

**Item 14 — Mummu shelves his own board ahead of his wipes, and The Deluge fires off a calculated self-summon (done, 2026-11-23).** Two gaps: the wipe scoring charged every own doomed card at full value with no Entropic Force alternative, so Mummu paid graveyard prices for bodies he could bank for mana; and The Deluge — which only resolves off a summon event — was never a planned line, so it fired only opportunistically at someone else's summon prompt and could never be sequenced shelf-first.

- **The wipe line prices the shelf.** In `CardStrategy.effect_score`'s mass-effect branch, an own doomed card now gains `bot._mummu_wipe_shelve_credit` on top of its `-value` charge whenever `action == null` (a wipe the bot casts itself — Famine, Earthquake, Fires of Judgment, Occult Singularity, Fall of the Mighty, The Deluge-by-self-summon; Bane petrifies instead of destroying, so nothing is saved ahead of it). The credit mirrors `_plan_mummu_entropic_force`'s doomed-target economics: the card banks into the deck bottom instead of the graveyard (`ECON_CARD_VALUE/2`) plus level-minus-one mana at the marginal rate. It is zero when Mummu cannot act (muted, not his priority), the card is not a legal Entropic Force target (face-down, immune to powers), or a destruction replacement already recovers the card somewhere better than the deck bottom (Draug Revenant's hand return) — those cards stay doomed at full charge.
- **Execution is shelf-first, brew-pattern style.** A wipe plan whose doom list contains a creditable own card carries `mummu_shelve_setup` (the wipe's uid); `_submit_strategy_plan` intercepts it and submits the next Entropic Force shelf in the plan's place, re-deriving the shelf list from the live board so a changed board cannot shelve a card that is no longer doomed. The next pass replays the line on the thinned board — the shelved card has left its doom list and its credit, so the accounting never double-counts, and the line's score actually rises as the shelves resolve (the full value charge drops while only the credit did).
- **Self-Deluge is a calculated line.** `_plan_self_deluge` prices summoning the cheapest legal hand trigger (creature, no sacrifice/discard/banish/shelve costs, face-up so the summon event fires, `_get_open_summon_lane_candidates` placement, Deluge still payable after the summon — shelf mana is banked into the budget first) and answering his own summon with the prepared Deluge. The base is the same wipe-branch score the priority prompt will produce (own doomed charges with shelf credits, minus the Deluge card and activation mana), minus the trigger's own doomed charge and summon mana, plus the same whole-doomed-set follower-safety term `_score_priority_card` adds. Deluges prepared this turn cannot respond yet and are excluded, as are hexes with special payment costs that would fight the shelf sequence. The plan joins the main-phase `plans` race and executes through the same `mummu_shelve_setup` interception: shelves → trigger summon → prepared Deluge at the summon-event prompt.
